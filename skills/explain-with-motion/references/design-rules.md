# Design rules for the motion panel

These rules come from real review rounds with a speaker/coach whose videos this format was built for.
Each one fixed something that looked fine to the builder and wrong to the person on camera.

## 1. The picture explains, the captions speak

The caption strip already carries every word. The panel should show what words cannot: structure,
movement, before/after, cause and effect. Text in the panel is allowed only when it adds something:

- a technical term the viewer should see written once (*Skeuomorphismus*, *Circle of Influence*)
- the one key sentence of the video, or a short formula (*Neu = Stress*, *Vertrautheit → Sicherheit*)
- the question or tool the viewer should take away (*Was ist vertraut? Was kenne ich daran schon?*)
- short labels on a diagram (circle names, *bisher / neu*, *Warum? Was? Wie? Wozu?*)

Do not put a sentence in the panel that repeats what is being said at that moment. When in doubt,
draw it instead.

## 2. No section headings

Small coloured overline headings above every scene ("THE QUESTION", "WHY FEAR ARISES", "IN THE ROOM")
read as clutter in a five-minute video. Leave them out. A source credit may stay, small and grey
(*nach Stephen Covey*).

## 3. The story is an example for a general principle

Speakers usually tell one concrete case to explain something general. The graphics should serve the
general point: no client names, no job titles from the anecdote (*"with the IT lead"*), no labels that
only make sense for that one company. Abstract the case: *bisher / neu* instead of the product
names, people figures instead of named roles. Keep concrete only what carries the idea (the S4 tile
stays because the whole story turns on it, but it is "the new thing", not a product).

## 4. Every phase gets a picture, the ending gets the strongest

The panel is always visible in a split screen, so an empty panel looks unfinished – even for two
seconds at the start of a scene. Let something enter within ~0.5 s of every scene start. Each phase of the
story gets a visual. The last third ("so what do we do now?") deserves the most imagery: the
method as a picture (columns with =, a pulse on the one new thing, a "+" that resolves the old
problem, a frame being built from four questions), and a closing image that calls back to the
beginning (the worried team from the start, now relaxed).

## 5. Diagrams must be logically correct

Viewers read placement as meaning. Everything sits where it belongs in the model:

- items sorted into a model sit inside the model, never outside its outer edge
- distribution matches the story (most items in *control* and *influence*, few in *concern*)
- the item the story is about sits in the zone the speaker assigns it to; ask if unsure
- labels sit inside their shape (*Vorher* inside its circle)
- labels never collide with lines, rings or other labels

Check snapshots at the moment each element lands, not only at the scene midpoint.

## 6. One visual language for the whole video

Pick 3-4 recurring motifs and reuse them: tiles (things/tasks), people figures (the team), rings and
circles (models, spheres), a stress line (jagged = stress, flat = calm). Recurring motifs let the
viewer follow the argument without labels. Colour has a job: primary = familiar/safe, accent = new /
the one thing to look at, muted = background.

## 7. Calm motion

Fades, short rises, gentle pops (scale 0.82 → 1, no bounce), lines that draw, one pulse to point.
Motion starts on the word it belongs to (± 0.2 s). Nothing jitters, nothing loops forever.
People figures may "boil" slightly (hand-drawn feel) while they are on screen.

## 8. Accessible and readable on a phone

Panel text ≥ 28 px (4:5/9:16 canvas), key formulas 70-120 px. Contrast of coloured text ≥ 4.5:1 –
`hyperframes check` reports it. Captions max two lines.

## 9. Sound

Keep the speaker's real voice. Clean-up (noise, reverb, levelling) is fine; voice re-synthesis
("studio voice" filters) makes people sound like someone else on normal call or camera audio.
Offer equally loud before/after samples and let the speaker choose.
